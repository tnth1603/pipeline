// Analytical Insights
01
Two-Wave Pattern
The outbreak has not followed a typical single-peak epidemic curve. Wave 1 (2022) was driven by MSM transmission in high-income countries. Wave 2 (2024–25) emerged from a new Clade Ib strain in Central Africa with different transmission dynamics — more severe, broader demographic impact.
02
Geographic Shift
Wave 1 was concentrated in USA, Spain, Germany, France, UK — high-income nations. Wave 2 shifted dramatically toward Sub-Saharan Africa: DRC (34,011), Uganda (8,512), Sierra Leone (5,442), Burundi (4,682). Sierra Leone's CFR of 1.1% vs USA's 0.17% reflects healthcare access disparity.
03
CFR Inequality
Case fatality rates vary 6x between high-income and low-income nations. Colombia, Germany, France, UK all show 0% recorded deaths — likely reflecting better access to antivirals (tecovirimat) and supportive care. Sierra Leone (1.1%), Uganda (0.61%), Mexico (0.82%) reflect treatment access gaps.
04
Endemicity Risk
2023 saw a low of 9,653 annual cases, suggesting potential control. However 2024 reversed this with 26,308 cases, and 2025 reached 53,818 — nearly matching 2022 levels. The disease appears to have established endemic transmission in parts of Central/East Africa, making global eradication unlikely in the near term.
05
Per Capita Burden
Population-adjusted analysis reveals a very different picture to raw counts. Sierra Leone leads with 590 cases per million, followed by Burundi (330), DRC (304), and Liberia (288). The USA, despite having the most raw cases, ranks much lower per capita. This reframes the narrative around where intervention is most needed.
06
Current Trajectory
The most recent data (Q1 2026) shows monthly cases declining from the 2025 peak: 1,711 in January, 1,477 in February, 1,170 in March. This suggests Wave 2 may be subsiding, though remaining above pre-2024 endemic baseline. Continued surveillance is critical to detect any potential Wave 3.
